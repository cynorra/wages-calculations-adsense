## Pre-Tax Deductions vs. Post-Tax Withholdings

Understanding the difference between pre-tax and post-tax payroll deductions is critical for maximizing household wealth:

### Pre-Tax Deductions (Lower Taxable Base)
Pre-tax deductions are subtracted from gross pay *before* federal and state income taxes are calculated. Examples include:
- Traditional 401(k) / 403(b) retirement plans
- Health Savings Accounts (HSA) and Flexible Spending Accounts (FSA)
- Section 125 employee health, dental, and vision insurance premiums

If an employee in the 22% marginal tax bracket contributes $500 per month ($6,000/yr) to a pre-tax 401(k), their federal income tax drops by $1,320 ($6,000 × 0.22). Consequently, contributing $500 only reduces net take-home pay by **$390**, effectively granting a $110 monthly subsidy from the federal government.

### Post-Tax Deductions
Post-tax deductions (such as Roth 401k contributions, union dues, disability insurance, or wage garnishments) are deducted *after* all federal, FICA, and state taxes have been computed, providing no immediate tax relief on the current paycheck.
