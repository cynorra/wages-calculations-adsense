## Biweekly (26 Paychecks) vs. Semi-Monthly (24 Paychecks)

A fundamental distinction in corporate payroll accounting lies between biweekly and semi-monthly pay cadences:
- **Biweekly (Every 2 Weeks):** Employees receive a paycheck every 14 days, resulting in **26 paychecks per calendar year** (52 weeks / 2 = 26).
- **Semi-Monthly (Twice a Month):** Employees are paid on specific calendar dates (usually the 1st and 15th, or 15th and last day of the month), totaling **24 paychecks per year**.

### The Math of "3-Paycheck Months"
Because there are 12 calendar months and 26 biweekly pay periods:
`26 pay periods - (12 months × 2 paychecks) = 2 extra paychecks`

Every calendar year contains exactly **two months in which a biweekly worker receives three paychecks** instead of two. Because most fixed household expenses (mortgage/rent, car payment, utilities, health insurance) occur on a monthly schedule, these two extra checks represent 100% discretionary or debt-accelerating capital.
