## Evaluating the True Value of an Hourly Raise

When negotiating compensation, employees often evaluate raises in simple hourly terms: "+$2.00 per hour." 

### Gross Annual Value of an Hourly Raise (2,080 Hours)
- **+$0.50 / hour:** $1,040.00 / year
- **+$1.00 / hour:** $2,080.00 / year
- **+$2.00 / hour:** $4,160.00 / year
- **+$3.00 / hour:** $6,240.00 / year
- **+$5.00 / hour:** $10,400.00 / year

### Net Take-Home Retention
Because the incremental dollars from a raise are taxed at your highest marginal tax bracket:
- If a worker in the 22% marginal tax bracket with 7.65% FICA receives a **$2.00/hr raise** ($4,160 gross):
  - Federal Withholding (22%): $915.20
  - FICA (7.65%): $318.24
  - Net Annual Gain: **$2,926.56** ($112.56 per biweekly paycheck)
  - Real Net Hourly Increase: **$1.41 / hour**

### Nominal vs. Real Wage Growth (Inflation)
If consumer prices (CPI) rise by 3.2% in a year, a worker earning $25.00/hour requires a raise of at least **+$0.80/hour** ($25 × 0.032) simply to maintain equal purchasing power. Any raise below $0.80 represents a net decrease in real living standards.
