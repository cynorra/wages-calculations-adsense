## The FLSA Three-Prong Exemption Test

To be legally exempt from overtime under Section 13(a)(1) of the FLSA, an employee must satisfy **all three** of the following tests:

1. **Salary Basis Test:** The employee must be paid a predetermined, fixed salary that cannot be reduced based on the quality or quantity of work performed.
2. **Salary Level Test:** The salary must meet or exceed the federal statutory threshold (currently $844 per week, or $43,888 annually, with higher thresholds established in recent DOL rulemakings).
3. **Duties Test:** The employee's primary job responsibilities must involve executive, administrative, professional, or outside sales functions as defined in 29 CFR Part 541.

### Misclassification Risk
Job titles alone do not determine exemption. Calling a worker an "Assistant Store Manager" or "Lead Coordinator" does not make them exempt if their primary duties consist of routine non-exempt tasks (stocking shelves, operating cash registers, clerical data entry). Misclassified employees are entitled to retroactive overtime back pay plus liquidated damages.

### Salaried Non-Exempt Employees
An employee can receive a fixed salary while remaining non-exempt. Salaried non-exempt workers receive their base salary for standard hours, but **must receive 1.5x overtime compensation** for all hours worked beyond 40 in a workweek.
