## The FLSA 7-Minute Rounding Rule (29 CFR § 785.48)

Federal regulations permit employers to record employee starting and stopping times to the nearest 5 minutes, 6 minutes (tenth of an hour), or 15 minutes (quarter of an hour), provided that the rounding scheme is neutral and averages out fairly over time.

### The 15-Minute Quarter-Hour Interval Matrix:
- **1 to 7 minutes:** Rounds down to the previous 15-minute mark (e.g. 8:07 AM rounds to 8:00 AM).
- **8 to 14 minutes:** Rounds up to the next 15-minute mark (e.g. 8:08 AM rounds to 8:15 AM).

### Legal Restrictions: Rounding Must Not Cheat Workers
Under federal law, rounding cannot operate solely to the employer's advantage. An employer cannot round an arrival at 8:07 AM up to 8:15 AM (costing the employee 8 minutes) while also rounding a departure at 5:07 PM down to 5:00 PM (costing the employee another 7 minutes). Over time, the rounding policy must result in compensation for all hours actually worked.

### Meal Breaks: Bona Fide vs. Compensable Time
Under **29 CFR § 785.19**, bona fide meal periods (typically 30 minutes or more) are not worktime and are unpaid. However, the employee must be completely relieved from duty. If an administrative assistant is required to answer phones or monitor a front desk while eating lunch, the break is legally compensable worktime.
