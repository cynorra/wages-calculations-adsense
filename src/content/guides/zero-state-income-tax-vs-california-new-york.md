## The 9 States with No Personal Income Tax

Nine US states levy no traditional personal income tax on earned wages:
**Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire (interest & dividends only), South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming.**

On an $100,000 gross salary, living in Texas or Florida saves an employee between $4,000 and $7,000 per year in state wage withholdings compared to California or New York.

### The Compensating Factors: Property & Sales Taxes
State governments require revenue to fund infrastructure, public education, and emergency services. States lacking income taxes frequently compensate through other taxation channels:
- **Texas Property Taxes:** Texas has one of the highest effective property tax rates in the nation (often 1.8% to 2.2% of assessed home value). On a $400,000 home, annual property taxes can reach $8,000 to $9,000.
- **Tennessee Sales Taxes:** Tennessee levies a combined state and local sales tax rate averaging 9.55%, taxing groceries and consumer goods at nation-leading rates.

### The True Net Comparison
For high earners (wages over $150,000), zero-income-tax states provide undeniable cash flow advantages. For middle-income workers with substantial home values, the net difference in total tax burden is often narrower than paycheck withholdings suggest.
