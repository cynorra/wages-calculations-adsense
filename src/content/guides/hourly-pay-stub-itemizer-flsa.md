## Statutory Pay Stub Requirements: Federal and State Recordkeeping Mandates

Every pay period, millions of American workers receive an earnings statement—commonly known as a **pay stub**—accompanying their physical paycheck or electronic direct deposit notification. While many glance only at the final "Net Pay" figure, a pay stub is a legally binding disclosure document governed by federal and state labor standards.

At the federal level, Section 11(c) of the Fair Labor Standards Act (**29 U.S.C. § 211(c)**) and Department of Labor regulations codified at **29 C.F.R. Part 516** mandate that every covered employer must maintain comprehensive records of hours worked, wages paid, and deductions made for each employee. 

While federal law requires employers to *keep* these records, individual states enforce strict statutory mandates requiring employers to *provide* an **accurate, itemized written wage statement** at the time of each wage payment:

- **Strict Itemized Statement States:** States such as California (Labor Code § 226), New York (Wage Theft Prevention Act), Illinois, Massachusetts, and Washington require mandatory itemized pay stubs detailing exact hours, rates, deductions, and legal employer entities.
- **Paystub Penalty Statues:** In California, an employer who knowingly and intentionally fails to provide an accurate, itemized pay stub faces statutory penalties under **Labor Code § 226(e)** of $50 for the initial pay period violation and $100 per employee for each subsequent violation, up to a maximum aggregate penalty of **$4,000 per employee**, plus mandatory attorney's fees.

## Anatomy of an Hourly Pay Stub: Deciphering the Acronyms

A standard corporate pay stub generated by payroll software (such as ADP, Workday, Paylocity, or Quickbooks) is split into five distinct functional sections:

### 1. General Header and Identification Data
- **Pay Period Start and End Dates:** The exact 7-day or 14-day calendar window during which labor was performed.
- **Pay Date:** The actual date funds are legally disbursed or transferred via Automated Clearing House (ACH).
- **Employee Identification:** Name, address, and the last 4 digits of the Social Security Number (masked under privacy laws).
- **Employer Legal Entity:** The registered legal business name, physical corporate address, and Federal Employer Identification Number (EIN).

---

### 2. Earnings and Hours Section
This section records how gross earnings were derived from timecard punches:

| Paystub Label | Formal Accounting Meaning | Description & Statutory Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **REG / BASE** | Regular Straight Time | Standard hours worked up to 40 per workweek, multiplied by the agreed base hourly rate. |
| **OVT / OT 1.5** | Overtime Premium (FLSA § 7) | Hours worked exceeding 40 in a workweek, compensated at not less than 1.5 times the regular rate. |
| **DBL / 2.0X** | Double Time | Premium pay for hours exceeding 12 in a single day or 7th consecutive workday (mandated under CA Labor Code § 510). |
| **DIFF / SHFT**| Shift Differential | Premium additions (e.g., +$3.50/hr) for evening, night, or weekend operational shifts. |
| **PTO / VAC** | Paid Time Off / Vacation | Non-working hours paid under company leave policies (not counted toward the 40-hour overtime threshold). |
| **HOL** | Holiday Pay | Compensation for observed federal or company holidays. |
| **COMM / BON** | Commission or Non-Discretionary Bonus| Incentive pay that must be integrated into the regular rate for overtime calculations. |

---

### 3. Tax Withholding Section (Statutory Deductions)
This section itemizes mandatory taxes subtracted by government authority:

| Tax Code / Acronym | Full Program Title | Federal / State Authority | Standard Tax Rate Formula |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **FED W/H / FIT** | Federal Income Tax Withholding | Internal Revenue Code § 3402 | Calculated using IRS Pub 15-T and employee Form W-4 elections. |
| **FICA SS / OASDI**| Social Security (Old-Age, Survivors & Disability)| Internal Revenue Code § 3101(a)| Flat 6.20% on gross wages up to the annual wage cap ($176,100). |
| **FICA MED / HI** | Medicare Hospital Insurance | Internal Revenue Code § 3101(b)| Flat 1.45% on all gross compensation with zero ceiling. |
| **ADD MED / MED2**| Additional Medicare Surtax | IRC § 3101(b)(2) | 0.90% levied on wages exceeding $200,000. |
| **ST W/H / SIT** | State Income Tax Withholding | State Department of Revenue | Varies from 0.00% (TX, FL, WA) to progressive state rates. |
| **CITY / LOCAL** | Municipal Income Tax | Local City Tax Ordinance | Imposed in cities like New York City, Philadelphia, or Detroit. |
| **SDI / TDI / SUI**| State Disability / Paid Leave | State Employment Development Dept| Employee-funded mandatory disability/family leave (e.g., CA SDI, NY PFL). |

---

### 4. Voluntary Pre-Tax & Post-Tax Deductions
- **401K / PRE-TAX:** Traditional employee retirement contributions (reduces FIT and SIT taxable base).
- **ROTH 401K / POST-TAX:** After-tax retirement savings (no immediate tax reduction).
- **SEC 125 / MED INS:** Employee portion of health insurance premiums under a Section 125 cafeteria plan (exempt from FIT, SIT, and FICA).
- **HSA / PRE-TAX:** Health Savings Account contributions (triple tax-advantaged).
- **GARN / LEVY:** Legally mandated post-tax wage garnishments for child support, tax liens, or court judgments.

---

### 5. Year-to-Date (YTD) Cumulative Totals
Every legitimate pay stub maintains two parallel accounting columns: **Current Period** and **Year-to-Date (YTD)**.
- **YTD Gross:** Total cumulative gross wages earned from January 1 through the current pay period.
- **YTD Deductions:** Total cumulative taxes and benefit withholdings across the calendar year.
- **YTD Net:** Total cumulative spendable cash disbursed to the worker year-to-date.

## Pay Stub Audit Checklist: How to Detect Payroll Errors

Workers should audit their pay stubs each pay period against this 4-step checklist:

### Step 1: Verify Straight Time and Overtime Hour Segregation
Confirm that regular hours and overtime hours are listed on separate lines with their respective hourly rates. If you worked 48 hours, ensure 40 hours appear under REG and 8 hours appear under OVT at 1.5x your regular rate. If all 48 hours are lumped into REG, your employer has committed an overtime violation.

### Step 2: Validate the FICA Tax Percentages
Perform a quick mathematical check:
- Multiply your FICA-taxable wages by **0.062**. The result should match FICA SS to the penny.
- Multiply your FICA-taxable wages by **0.0145**. The result should match FICA MED to the penny.
- If the numbers diverge significantly, your payroll department has misconfigured your pre-tax deduction exemptions.

### Step 3: Monitor the Social Security Wage Cap
If your YTD Gross approaches or exceeds the annual Social Security cap ($176,100 in 2026), verify that the 6.2% FICA SS deduction ceases completely for all remaining paychecks in that calendar year.

### Step 4: Reconcile PTO Accruals and Balances
Many states require pay stubs to display current available Paid Time Off (PTO), sick leave balances, and accrued hours earned during the pay period. Verify that PTO hours deducted reflect actual time taken off.
