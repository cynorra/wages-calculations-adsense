## The Persistent Bracket Myth

One of the most damaging misconceptions in personal finance is the belief that getting an hourly pay raise (for example, from $23.00/hr to $25.00/hr) might push you into a "higher tax bracket" and cause your net take-home pay to decrease.

### Mathematical Proof of Marginal Taxation
The United States employs a **strictly progressive marginal tax bracket system**. Brackets function like water buckets:
- The first $15,000 you earn (Standard Deduction) is taxed at **0%**.
- Income from $15,001 to $26,925 is taxed at **10%**.
- Income from $26,926 to $63,475 is taxed at **12%**.
- Income from $63,476 to $118,350 is taxed at **22%**.

When an hourly raise causes annual income to rise from $60,000 to $65,000, only the $1,525 above the $63,475 threshold is taxed at 22%. The prior $48,475 of taxable income remains taxed at the lower 10% and 12% rates. **It is mathematically impossible for an hourly raise to reduce your total take-home pay.**
