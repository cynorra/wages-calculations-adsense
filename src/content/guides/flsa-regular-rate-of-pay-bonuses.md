## The Most Common Employer Payroll Violation

Under **29 U.S.C. § 207(e)** and **29 CFR § 778.208**, non-discretionary bonuses (such as productivity bonuses, quality incentives, attendance awards, or commissions) must be included in the employee's regular rate before overtime is calculated.

### Scenario: The Illegal Calculation vs. The Legal FLSA Method
Suppose an employee earns a base rate of $20.00/hour, works 50 hours in a week, and receives a $100 attendance/production bonus.

#### 1. The Common (Illegal) Calculation:
- Straight Time (50 hrs × $20.00) = $1,000
- Overtime (10 hrs × $20.00 × 0.5) = $100
- Bonus = $100
- **Total Paid: $1,200.00** *(Employer underpays the worker!)*

#### 2. The FLSA Statutory Calculation:
1. **Total Straight-Time Earnings:** (50 hrs × $20.00) + $100 bonus = $1,100.00
2. **Statutory Regular Rate:** $1,100 / 50 hours = **$22.00 / hour** (NOT $20.00!)
3. **Overtime Premium Rate:** $22.00 × 0.5 = $11.00 / hour
4. **Overtime Compensation:** 10 overtime hours × $11.00 = $110.00
5. **Total Lawful Gross Pay:** $1,100 + $110 = **$1,210.00**

Omitting the bonus from the regular rate deprives the employee of legal overtime compensation and creates significant back-wage liabilities during Department of Labor (DOL) wage and hour audits.
