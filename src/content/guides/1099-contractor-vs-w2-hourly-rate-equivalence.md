## The W-2 to 1099 Rate Multiplier

A common career dilemma involves leaving a W-2 salaried or hourly position to become a 1099 independent contractor. Transitioning at the same nominal hourly rate results in an immediate **25% to 35% reduction in net income**.

### 1. The 15.3% Self-Employment Tax (SECA) Shift
On a W-2 paycheck:
- The employer pays 6.2% Social Security + 1.45% Medicare.
- The employee pays 6.2% Social Security + 1.45% Medicare.

As a 1099 contractor, you must pay **both halves** via the Self-Employment Contributions Act (SECA), totaling **15.3%** on net business earnings.

### 2. Factoring Benefits and Unpaid Downtime
A W-2 employee receiving $35.00/hour often receives:
- Health insurance subsidy ($500/month = ~$3.00/hr)
- 15 days of PTO + 10 paid holidays = 200 paid unworked hours (~$3.50/hr)
- 401(k) match (4% = $1.40/hr)

To equal a **$35.00/hour W-2 position**, an independent contractor must charge at least **$50.00 to $55.00 per hour** to cover self-employment taxes, private health insurance, unbillable business overhead, and unpaid vacation.
