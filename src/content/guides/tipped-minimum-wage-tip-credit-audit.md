## The FLSA Tip Credit Mechanism: Statutory Authority (29 U.S.C. § 203(m))

In the American hospitality, restaurant, bar, and personal service industries, compensation is uniquely structured around customer gratuities. Under Section 3(m) of the Fair Labor Standards Act (**29 U.S.C. § 203(m)**) and Department of Labor regulations (**29 C.F.R. Part 531**), employers are permitted to take an accounting credit toward their federal minimum wage obligations based on the tips their employees receive:

$$\text{Direct Cash Wage} + \text{Tip Credit Claimed} = \text{Applicable Statutory Minimum Wage}$$

At the federal level, where the standard minimum wage has remained fixed at **$7.25 per hour** since 2009:
- **Statutory Direct Cash Wage Floor:** **$2.13 per hour**
- **Maximum Allowable Federal Tip Credit:** **$5.12 per hour** ($7.25 - $2.13)

Under this statutory framework, an employer can pay a bartender, server, or busser as little as $2.13 per hour in direct payroll compensation, provided the employee receives at least $5.12 per hour in tips to reach the statutory $7.25 baseline.

## The Mandatory Employer "Make-Up Pay" Obligation

A critical statutory rule codified in **29 C.F.R. § 531.59(b)** is that an employer's tip credit is **strictly contingent on actual customer tips received**:

$$\mathbf{\text{If Direct Cash Wage} + \text{Actual Tips} < \text{Minimum Wage, the Employer Must Pay the Shortfall.}}$$

Tipped wages are audited on a **workweek-by-workweek basis**. An employer cannot average earnings across multiple weeks to avoid make-up pay obligations.

### Audit Simulation: Slow Winter Workweek
Consider a restaurant server in a state observing the federal $7.25 baseline:
- **Hours Worked in Workweek:** 35 hours
- **Direct Employer Cash Wages ($2.13/hr):** $35 \times \$2.13 = \$74.55$
- **Total Customer Tips Received:** $100.00 (Due to severe blizzards and cancelled reservations)
- **Total Compensation Realized:** $\$74.55 + \$100.00 = \mathbf{\$174.55}$
- **Effective Realized Hourly Rate:** $\frac{\$174.55}{35 \text{ hours}} = \mathbf{\$4.99 \text{ per hour}}$

### The Legal Remedy (Make-Up Cash):
- Federal Minimum Wage Requirement (35 hrs × $7.25): **$253.75**
- Actual Total Received: **$174.55**
- **Mandatory Employer Make-Up Payment:** $\$253.75 - \$174.55 = \mathbf{\$79.20}$

The employer must disburse an additional **$79.20 on that week's paycheck** to bring total earnings up to the legal $7.25 per hour floor. Failure to issue make-up pay constitutes an illegal minimum wage violation under FLSA § 6.

## 50-State Landscape: The 3 Regulatory Models

While federal law permits a $2.13 direct cash wage, states have established vastly different wage protections. Tipped wage laws across the nation fall into three distinct regulatory models:

| Regulatory Category | Representative States | Statutory Rules & Cash Wage Floor | Tip Credit Allowed? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tier 1: Equal Treatment (Zero Tip Credit)** | California, Washington, Oregon, Nevada, Montana, Alaska, Minnesota | Employers must pay the **full state minimum wage** in direct cash before tips. In CA ($16.50+) and WA ($16.66+), servers receive full minimum wage + 100% of tips. | **NO (100% BANNED)** |
| **Tier 2: Higher Direct Cash Wage** | New York, Florida, Illinois, Massachusetts, Colorado, Arizona | State sets a minimum wage higher than federal, and requires a direct cash wage significantly above $2.13 (e.g., NY $10.65+ cash wage, FL $9.98+ cash wage). | **YES (Restricted Credit)** |
| **Tier 3: Federal Baseline ($2.13)** | Texas, Georgia, Alabama, Tennessee, Louisiana, Indiana, Utah | Adheres strictly to the federal statutory floor of $2.13 cash wage and $5.12 tip credit. | **YES (Max $5.12 Credit)** |

### Economic Impact on Hospitality Workers:
A restaurant server working 40 hours per week in Seattle, Washington earns **$666.40 per week ($16.66/hr) in guaranteed employer cash wages** before receiving any tips. An identical server performing the same duties in Dallas, Texas earns **$85.20 per week ($2.13/hr) in employer cash wages**, relying almost entirely upon guest tips for basic survival.

## Overtime Calculations for Tipped Employees: The $2.13 Trap

A widespread payroll violation in the restaurant sector involves miscalculating overtime for tipped staff.

Many restaurant managers mistakenly believe that overtime for a server earning $2.13/hr is simply $2.13 × 1.5 = $3.20/hr. **This is completely illegal under 29 C.F.R. § 778.111.**

### The Lawful Tipped Overtime Formula:
Under federal regulations, the 1.5x overtime multiplier **must be applied to the full statutory minimum wage**, not the reduced cash wage. The allowable tip credit ($5.12) is then subtracted from the overtime rate:

$$\text{Overtime Hourly Cash Wage} = (\text{Statutory Minimum Wage} \times 1.5) - \text{Tip Credit Claimed}$$

### Step-by-Step Federal Calculation:
1. Full Minimum Wage: $7.25 / hour
2. Full Overtime Rate (1.5x): $\$7.25 \times 1.5 = \mathbf{\$10.875 \text{ per hour}}$
3. Subtract Allowable Tip Credit: $\$10.875 - \$5.12 = \mathbf{\$5.755 \approx \$5.76 \text{ per hour}}$

$$\mathbf{\text{The Employer Must Pay At Least \$5.76 per Hour in Direct Cash for All Overtime Hours.}}$$

Paying $3.20/hr instead of $5.76/hr cheats the employee out of **$2.56 for every overtime hour worked**, creating severe collective action liability during Department of Labor wage audits.

## Tip Pooling and Tip Sharing: Strict Federal Boundaries

Under amendments to the FLSA and 2021 Department of Labor regulations codified at **29 C.F.R. Part 531 Subpart D**:

### 1. When the Employer Takes a Tip Credit ($2.13 Cash Wage)
- The tip pool can **only include customarily and regularly tipped front-of-house staff** (servers, food runners, bussers, bartenders).
- Back-of-house staff (cooks, dishwashers, janitors) are **strictly barred** from participating in the tip pool.
- If an employer forces servers to share tips with line cooks while claiming a tip credit, the employer **forfeits the tip credit entirely** for all staff and owes full minimum wage back pay to every employee.

### 2. When the Employer Pays Full Minimum Wage (No Tip Credit)
- Under the 2018 Consolidated Appropriations Act amendments, if an employer pays all employees the full minimum wage in direct cash (claiming zero tip credit), the employer **may include non-tipped back-of-house employees (cooks, dishwashers)** in a valid mandatory tip pool.

### 3. Managers and Supervisors CAN NEVER Keep Employee Tips
Under **29 U.S.C. § 203(m)(2)(B)**, managers and supervisors (defined under the executive exemption duties test) are **strictly prohibited from keeping any portion of employee tips or participating in tip pools**, regardless of whether a tip credit is claimed. Managers may only keep tips given to them directly by customers for service they directly and solely provided without assistance.

## The 80/20/30 Rule: Non-Tipped Side Work Protections

Under Department of Labor dual-jobs regulations (**29 C.F.R. § 531.56(f)**), servers frequently perform non-tipped side duties such as rolling silverware, making coffee, slicing lemons, or stocking wait stations:

- **Directly Supporting Work:** Non-tipped side work directly related to tipped occupation is compensable at the tip credit rate **only if it does not exceed 20% of the employee's workweek**.
- **The 30-Minute Continuous Rule:** If a server spends **more than 30 continuous minutes** performing side work (e.g., coming in an hour before opening to clean restrooms and prep kitchens), the employer cannot claim a tip credit for that time and **must pay full statutory minimum wage in direct cash**.
