## The FLSA Tip Credit Mechanism (29 U.S.C. § 203(m))

Under federal labor standards, an employer may pay a direct cash wage as low as **$2.13 per hour** to a tipped employee, claiming a **maximum tip credit of $5.12 per hour** against the $7.25 federal minimum wage:

`Direct Cash Wage ($2.13) + Tip Credit ($5.12) = Federal Minimum Wage ($7.25)`

### Mandatory Employer Make-Up Requirement
If an employee's direct cash wage plus actual tips received does not equal at least $7.25 per hour across the workweek, **the employer must pay the difference directly**. An employee cannot legally earn less than minimum wage in any workweek.

### State Law Divergence: Tip Credit Bans
Seven states completely prohibit tip credits: **California, Washington, Oregon, Nevada, Montana, Alaska, and Minnesota**. In these states, employers must pay the full statutory state minimum wage (e.g. $16.50/hr in California) before the worker receives a single dollar in tips.

### Tip Pooling Restrictions
Under 2021 DOL regulations, employers who take a tip credit cannot include non-tipped employees (dishwashers, cooks) or supervisors/managers in tip pools. If an employer does not take a tip credit and pays the full minimum wage in direct cash, back-of-house staff may participate in tip pools.
